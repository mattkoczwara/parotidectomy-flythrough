parotid-duct-course established-anatomy sources checked clinical review: pending

Essentials: Saliva leaves the gland through a duct that runs forward across the chewing muscle (masseter) and passes through the cheek muscle (buccinator) into the mouth.

Anatomy: The parotid (Stensen's) duct leaves the anterior border of the gland, crosses the lateral surface of the masseter, penetrates the buccinator and opens in the mouth beside the second upper molar. In a cadaveric study it lay slightly above the line from the tragus to the mouth corner at the masseter's anterior border in most specimens.

Clinical: At the anterior border of the masseter the duct was slightly superior to the tragus–mouth-corner line in 84.4% of 32 hemifaces, on the line in 3.1% and below it in 12.5% (most of those with an accessory gland); it pierced the buccinator medial to the vertical through the lateral canthus in 87.5%.

Duct slightly above the tragus–mouth-corner line at the masseter’s anterior border: 84.4 %; Cadaver hemifaces from 16 adults (mean age 82; 15 Korean, 1 Caucasian), 27 of 32 hemifaces; Cadaveric dissection study

Limitations: Sixteen elderly cadavers, almost all Korean; the course is described relative to surface landmarks and varies between people. The model draws the duct to the buccinator only.

Essentials: Some people have a small separate piece of gland tissue in front of the main parotid, resting on the duct.

Anatomy: An accessory parotid gland, separate from the main gland, is a common variant. When present it can complicate parotidectomy and can be the site of tumours.

Clinical: Pooled prevalence of an accessory parotid gland was 32.1% (95% CI 21.2–44.0), higher in cadaveric studies (35.8%) than on CT (21.5%); when present it was mostly unilateral (77.8%). In 19 dissected glands it formed ESGS level V in 9 and under 5% of the gland’s weight.

Essentials: A superficial parotidectomy removes the whole outer lobe of the gland, the part that lies outside the facial nerve.

Essentials: Most comparisons between operations come from records of what surgeons chose, so they cannot show that one operation is safer than another for the same tumour.

Anatomy: No randomised trial has compared extracapsular dissection with superficial parotidectomy. The studies are retrospective, differ in size, follow-up and tumour mix, and rarely say how the operation was chosen, which introduces selection bias.

Clinical: The UK national guideline asks only that benign parotid tumours be completely excised, which offers good cure rates.

Limitations: Salzano 2025 is itself a single-arm pooled analysis of mostly retrospective studies with some internally inconsistent figures.
